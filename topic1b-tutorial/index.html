What to notice

  1. Control at 0 V: the lamp is off. Which region, and how are both junctions biased?
  2. Control at 5 V with plenty of base drive: the lamp is fully on. Which region now?
  3. Turn the base drive down while ON until the lamp dims. Why does the transistor get hot?

Model: lamp 28 V, 100 mA (280 Ω), β = 100. Full brightness needs IB ≥ 1 mA. Underdrive it and the transistor sits in the active region with a lot of voltage across it, so it dissipates VCE × IC as heat. A good switch avoids the active region.

Station 4 · BJT or JFET? A BJT is controlled by a current: it has to draw base current. A JFET is controlled by a voltage: its gate draws almost nothing. Drive the JFET, then connect a weak sensor to each one and see which transistor can read it.

ID = IDSS(1 − VGS/VGS(off))², with IDSS = 10 mA and VGS(off) = −4 V. The gate–source junction is reverse biased, so the gate current is only leakage: nanoamps or less.
